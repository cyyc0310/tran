# V344 research positioning after current China information-budget experiments

Date: 2026-10-30. This is a focused primary-literature audit, not a full systematic review.

## Closest current China work

**Li et al., Scientific Data (2026), “High temporal and spatial resolution projected electricity carbon emission factors of China from 2025–2060,” DOI [10.1038/s41597-026-07272-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-07272-6).** The paper publishes 8,760 hourly projections for 31 provinces, eight future years and five planning scenarios. It combines a generation/transmission/load/storage planning model, hourly operation model and carbon-flow calculation. It validates the generated series against official annual provincial factors for 2021–2023 (reported MAPE up to 2.525%) and validates internal hourly carbon-flow balance. The hourly dataset is public through [Figshare](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.28953545). The GTSL/PSOS planning and operation code is proprietary/not shared; the authors state that no hourly projected-factor dataset was available for intercomparison at publication. This is a close competing resource, but it is a long-horizon physical planning projection rather than a release-time statistical forecast for a previously unmeasured province.

**Lyu et al., SSRN preprint (posted 12 Aug 2026), “Hourly consumption-based carbon intensity reveals spatiotemporal patterns and load-carbon mismatch in China's high-renewable power system,” [SSRN abstract 7268249](https://ssrn.com/abstract=7268249).** It combines a provincial hourly load model, PyPSA-China planning/operation and carbon-flow accounting to produce prospective hourly consumption-based intensities and examine transmission/load mismatch. It is a recent preprint, not peer reviewed at the time of this audit. It reinforces that demand and interprovincial flow matter for consumption-side CIF; the five NBS generation totals cannot alone reproduce this estimand.

**Wang et al., SSRN preprint (posted 24 Dec 2025), “A Machine Learning Framework for High-Resolution Provincial Carbon Emission Factors in China’s Power Sector: Multi-Model Evaluation, SHAP Interpretation, and Scenario Analysis,” [SSRN abstract 5960745](https://ssrn.com/abstract=5960745).** This is the closest methodological neighbor found in this search. Its abstract disaggregates monthly *fuel-type* generation to daily values with meteorology and heating-period features, combines daily emissions from CarbonMonitor-China as training targets, and evaluates RF/GB/XGBoost/LightGBM with time-series cross-validation on three provinces (Inner Mongolia, Jiangsu, Yunnan). It reports SHAP and Monte Carlo scenarios. This substantially overlaps with “monthly-to-higher-frequency provincial CEF.” The remaining differentiation is a harder information contract: no local daily emission/CIF labels in the held-out province, complete spatial holdout, NBS technology-level rather than fuel-level margins, hour-level tail risk, and calibrated partial-identification/abstention. The page is an SSRN preprint; the accessible abstract does not establish a leave-one-province-out evaluation, so the claimed cross-province generalization should not be assumed to prove zero-label regional transfer.

## Adjacent method literature

**Zhang et al., arXiv preprint 2601.06530 (2026), “Improving Day-Ahead Grid Carbon Intensity Forecasting by Joint Modeling of Local-Temporal and Cross-Variable Dependencies Across Different Frequencies.”** It proposes wavelet-kernel multiscale and dynamic cross-variable modules, evaluated on four Australian markets with hourly CIF, demand, renewable/nonrenewable generation and temperature inputs. This does not answer the low-frequency, no-local-hourly-CIF information contract in TransCIF. Wavelets may help once dense temporal signals exist; adding them to monthly margins would add unsupported model complexity.

Temporal aggregation in power-system planning is a mature area; e.g. [Merrick (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eneco.2016.08.001) shows that representation error grows sharply when wind/solar variability is aggregated, compared with demand-only representation. This strengthens the physical rationale for expecting a large residual hourly-shape set under monthly information, but it does not supply an emissions-tail identification method.

## Evidence from TransCIF V341–V343

The NBS monthly technology groups plus retrospective CEC installed capacity produce sharp model-conditional integer K ranges for 330 complete 2023 province-periods. Capacity narrows the mean τ=300 range by about 14%; narrowing the assumed thermal EF support from 400–1,000 to an oracle point at 700 narrows it by another ~182 hours when capacity is present, but by only 0.4 hours without capacity. Even with oracle EF and lagged capacity, the mean τ=300 range remains ~500 hours. These figures are not empirical hourly coverage: they quantify the consequences of declared composition, factor, denominator and capacity assumptions.

## Positioning decision

The current evidence does not justify presenting a new neural CIF predictor, a new wavelet method, or a new sharp-capacity algorithm. The mathematical construction is already present in the project's V92/V102 line; the China-specific contribution is the information-budget map and the tested interaction between public capacity and thermal-factor knowledge. A defensible potential paper is a **release-aware, information-budget benchmark for hourly CIF risk under regional data scarcity**, with four separated tracks:

1. **Point forecast track:** true as-of lagged information only, nested spatial holdouts, temporal blocks, region-balanced proper scores, empirical coverage and abstention.
2. **Partial-identification track:** exact/direct-K ranges from monthly technology margins, capacities and transparent emission-factor supports; keep this distinct from nominal prediction intervals.
3. **Model-projection track:** compare public high-resolution China projections (including Li et al.) as model outputs, not as hourly ground truth; distinguish annual calibration and internal physical consistency from hourly forecast validation.
4. **Adjacent daily-label track:** reproduce or protocol-match the Wang et al. monthly-to-daily framework where CarbonMonitor-China daily emissions are available; then test true held-out province transfer after removing all local daily emissions from fitting/calibration. Report that as a separate label-budget increment from the zero-local-label track.

The remaining novelty gate is whether this benchmark exposes a material and reproducible failure mode or minimum-information frontier that the existing China planning and monthly-to-daily studies cannot answer. We need matched estimands (generation vs consumption), as-of release dates, honest handling of annual official EF delay, and an independent hourly label benchmark outside China. Without those, the current work supports a methodological/data paper concept but not a claimed breakthrough in transferable hourly CIF prediction.
